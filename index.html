Section 1 • Agricultural Motivation

The Potato Disease Crisis & The Optical Vision Dilemma

Globally, 20–40% of agricultural crops are lost annually to pests and diseases. In Prince Edward Island, changing climate patterns threaten regional food security and farm livelihoods.

Early Blight (EB)

Alternaria solani • Fungal Pathogen
10% – 34% Yield Loss

Spread by wind and driving rain. Favored by alternating periods of high rainfall and warm temperature stress. Causes dark, concentric brown spots ("target-board" lesions) leading to premature defoliation and tuber rotting.

  • Primary Drivers: Farm location, cumulative precipitation & ambient temperature.
  • Spore Mechanics: Moderate spore production (mean 33 spores/m³, peak 2,099 spores/m³).
  • Seasonality: Spread consistently throughout the PEI growing season.

Gray Mold (GM)

Botrytis cinerea • Necrotrophic Fungus
20% – 50% Yield Loss

Devastating fungal infection producing fuzzy gray-brown mycelia. Thrives in cool-to-moderate temperatures under prolonged relative humidity and dense, poorly ventilated canopy microclimates.

  • Primary Drivers: Relative humidity (>85%), wind direction, temperature & dew point.
  • Spore Mechanics: Enormous spore volume (mean 422 spores/m³, peak 21,000 spores/m³).
  • Seasonality: High early-season volatility with rapid explosive surges.

The Core AI Bottleneck: Why Image-Only Deep Learning Is Not Enough

Most existing AI literature relies on image-based deep learning (MobileNetV2, ResNet50, YOLO, CNN ensembles) boasting >98% test set accuracy. However, this creates a critical agricultural paradox:

Late-Stage Optical Vision (Post-Infection)

CNNs require visible tissue necrosis or spots. Once leaf symptoms are visually discernible by a camera, fungal mycelia have already penetrated plant cell walls. By this stage, preventive fungicides are ineffective, requiring expensive curative systemic sprays that risk chemical resistance and yield forfeiture.

Pre-Symptomatic Spore ML (Pre-Infection)

By capturing airborne fungal spores (Airspore impaction collectors) and pairing them with microclimate weather data, machine learning predicts spore inoculation and germination days before physical leaf lesions appear. Farmers can deploy precision protective fungicide sprays within the optimal pre-infection window.

Section 7 • Discussion & Open Science

Discussion, Conclusions & Public Repository

Demonstrating how aerobiological pre-symptomatic modeling supports agricultural sustainability, optimizes fungicide expenditures, and prevents catastrophic food loss.

Headline Outcomes

By fusing biological spore counts with weather variables, pre-symptomatic prediction is demonstrated to be viable. Random Forest proved the most robust off-the-shelf classifier (90% Group accuracy, 95% Recall), Decision Trees offered interpretable decision boundaries (87% accuracy), and XGBoost matched RF after tuning (90% Group, 94% on Gray Mold).

Disease-specific models captured nuanced biological triggers better than group architectures—with Gray Mold benefiting most significantly.

Section 8 • Critical Evaluation

Points Worth Treating with Caution (Peer-Review Critical Analysis)

Essential scientific caveats, statistical nuances, and methodology checks identified from thorough peer analysis of the publication:

Inconsistent Reporting Figures

The manuscript conclusion claims Random Forest attained 92% accuracy for Early Blight, whereas Table 6 and the empirical results text state 82%. Additionally, the text notes 1,482 samples split as 741 per disease, while class-balance counts show 275 absence and 1,207 presence.

Possible Label Circularity

The binary target label is derived directly from spore counts (>0 = presence, 0 = absence). Thus, "predicting disease" is mechanically predicting airborne spore presence from meteorological variables. Spore presence and actual disease occurrence are used interchangeably without proving physical infection.

"Pre-Symptomatic" Claim Nuances

The study does not validate model predictions against confirmed physical leaf lesions or final harvest yield losses. Physical airborne spore presence indicates inoculum pressure in the air, but does not guarantee successful leaf cuticular penetration or lesion establishment.

The 81% Class Imbalance Baseline

With 1,207 positive instances out of 1,482 (81.4%), a naive dummy classifier predicting "present" 100% of the time achieves ~81.4% accuracy. This explains why Logistic Regression scored 83% accuracy while collapsing on absence cases (0 True Negatives and 11 False Positives for Gray Mold).

Validation Leakage Risks

Random stratified splits of weekly samples across the same 19 fields over 5 years introduce risk of temporal and spatial autocorrelation leakage. A rigorous leave-one-year-out or leave-one-field-out validation protocol would provide a stricter test of true generalization.

Disease Management & Action Advisory Protocol

Actionable steps to control the infection, prevent spreading, and save crop yield.

Emergency Containment Protocol

  • Isolate Infected Plants: Immediately remove and destroy severely infected leaves or stems to stop sporangia dispersion.
  • Halt Overhead Irrigation: Cease sprinkler watering immediately. Moisture on leaves accelerates fungal germination.
  • Sanitize Pruning Shears: Wipe tools with 70% isopropyl alcohol between plants to prevent mechanical transmission.
  • Ventilate Canopy: Prune dense lower foliage to increase air circulation around the crop base.

Biological & Eco-Friendly Management

  • Copper-Based Spray: Apply organic copper octanoate or Bordeaux mixture at early symptom onset.
  • Bacillus subtilis: Spray Bacillus subtilis strain QST 713 weekly to suppress fungal spore germination.
  • Neem Oil Extract: Apply cold-pressed neem oil (0.5% concentration) to coat leaf surfaces against spores.
  • Compost Tea Foliar Spray: Apply aerated compost tea to introduce beneficial competitive microbes.
